# C1.3 content brief (EF General English C1.3, Units 1 to 6)

Internal unit numbers continue after B2.3 (19-24): **C1.3 uses 25-30**. Level key "C1.3", prefix "c13".
All formats are IDENTICAL to the C1.2 module: read `c12/README.md` and copy the style of `c12/data/*.js`
exactly (same field order, separators, valid JS that runs in node). Variable names replace `_C12` with `_C13`
(e.g. `VOCAB_C13_RAW`, `GRAMMAR_C13`, `GX_C13_P1`, `CL_C13_4`). Grammar topic ids use the prefix `c13-`.
Also look at `b23/data/*.js` (the most recent module) for the newer `TR_B23` transformation format
(`TR_C13` here, 6 items per topic: {o, s, a:[...], w}).

Extra reference material in this folder: `book_excerpts.txt` (OCR of book pages, messy) and
`grammar_reference_ocr.txt` (OCR of the Grammar Reference pages 141-146).

Rules for all content
- Everything in English only (no Spanish, no translations).
- Level C1 (advanced). Writing tasks: 220-260 words.
- British spelling, except when quoting American usage on purpose.
- Examples: natural, varied; some (not all) set in Ecuador (Quito, Latacunga, Cotopaxi, Chimborazo, Galápagos, Amazon/Yasuní, Cuenca, Guayaquil) or electrical engineering / research / university life.
- Do NOT use long dashes (— or –) or arrows (→) anywhere. Use commas, colons, full stops or brackets.
- Thousands separator: full stop if a big number is ever needed (avoid where possible); in the numerical-data grammar topic you may write numbers in words or percentages.
- Pipe `|` must never appear inside a field. No backticks inside template strings.

## Grammar (two topics per unit, Grammar Reference pp. 141-146)

- 25 Lesson 2: **Punctuation of quotations** (single vs double quotation marks BrE/AmE; quotation within a quotation; commas and full stops outside (BrE) or inside (AmE); question and exclamation marks inside if part of the quote; colons and semicolons outside). Practice: choose the correctly punctuated version.
- 25 Lesson 5: **Verbs with two objects** (indirect + direct object; switching order with to/for: give/send/lend/show/offer to, buy/make/build/cook/find for; explain/describe/suggest/announce + to only (NOT explain you the procedure); pronoun as direct object: give it to me, not give me it in formal use).
- 26 Lesson 2: **Expressing numerical data** (percentages + of; subject-verb agreement with percentages; fractions: numerator cardinal, denominator ordinal, half, quarter; a/one third; one-fifteenth the amount, half as many, a tenth as much; ranges from...to / between...and; hundred/thousand/million not pluralised after a number, millions of; time frames a month, daily; adverbs of precision: about, roughly, on average, just under).
- 26 Lesson 5: **Viewpoint adverbs** (personally, frankly, ethically speaking, as far as X is concerned, in ethical terms, officially, apparently, obviously, presumably, surprisingly, understandably; position: front with comma, mid-position after auxiliary/be, end; adverbs judging behaviour after subject: She bravely decided...; certainty adverbs in mid-position: would definitely, presumably).
- 27 Lesson 2: **Focusing adverbs** (only, just, even, especially, particularly, mainly, mostly, largely, primarily, predominantly, purely, solely, singularly, simply; position changes meaning: Only they could... / They could only...; before the word they focus on; mid-position).
- 27 Lesson 5: **Question forms** (indirect questions: statement word order, if/whether, no question mark usually; subject vs object questions; declarative questions with rising intonation; echo/short questions: When? What for? Why me?; questions with that clauses: Who do you think (that) they'll cast?; interrogative expressions: What sort of, How far, How much).
- 28 Lesson 1: **Reflexive pronouns** (subject = object; emphasis: I did it myself, the director herself; by + reflexive = alone/without help; own as possessive reflexive: their own passion, on my own; verbs not usually reflexive in English: relax, concentrate, feel, meet, wash/dress (optional); each other vs themselves).
- 28 Lesson 5: **Possessives** ('s and s' with people, organisations, time (a week's holiday); of with things; whole phrases: the man at the station's report; double possessives: Angela's brother's team; possessive pronouns; its and one's with own; whose; that book of mine; several of the desert's characteristics; that of / those of in formal comparisons).
- 29 Lesson 2: **Uses of that** (determiner, demonstrative pronoun, relative pronoun (can be omitted when object), intensifier (not that important), conjunction after reporting and perception verbs (can be omitted), two-word conjunctions: so that, now that, given that, provided that, in that, except that).
- 29 Lesson 5: **Conjunctions** (coordinating FANBOYS; correlative: not only... but also (with inversion: Not only did we...), either... or, neither... nor, both... and, whether... or; if... then; subordinating: although, even if, whenever, whether or not, as soon as, since, until; conjunctive adverbs: nevertheless, conversely, however, consequently, hence, thus; punctuation: comma between independent clauses, Oxford comma, comma after a fronted subordinate clause, comma before whereas/although at the end, semicolon + conjunctive adverb + comma).
- 30 Lesson 2: **Comparative structures** (adjective + enough + infinitive; a lot / far / considerably + comparative; as if / as though + clause (as though it were); much / many more + noun; twice as... as; the more... the more; not nearly as... as; nowhere near as; every bit as; no less/more... than; the same as; similar to / different from).
- 30 Lesson 5: **Cleft sentences with it** (It is/was + emphasised element + that/who clause: It is primarily human activity that is the cause...; It's not big business that is responsible...; It is ordinary people who...; it-clefts with adverbials: It was in 2010 that...; It was only when... that; negative and question forms: Was it you who...?; contrast with what-clefts).
