Rules for all content
- Everything in English only (no Spanish, no translations).
- Level B2 (upper-intermediate), not C1. Writing tasks: 140-190 words.
- British spelling (savour, symbolise is ok but the book writes "symbolize"; keep book headwords as written).
- Examples: natural, varied; some (not all) set in Ecuador (Quito, Latacunga, Baños, Cuenca, Galápagos, Otavalo, Inti Raymi, Mama Negra in Latacunga, Año Viejo dolls on 31 December, Carnaval in Guaranda) or electrical engineering / university life.
- Do NOT use long dashes (— or –) or arrows (→) anywhere in the text. Use commas, colons, full stops or brackets instead.
- In numbers use a full stop for thousands if ever needed (avoid big numbers where possible).
- Pipe character `|` must never appear inside a field. Avoid backticks inside template strings.

## Grammar (two topics per unit, from the Grammar Reference)

- 19 Lesson 2: **Modals of speculation in the present and past** (must, may, might, could, can't + base form; must have, may have, might have, could have, can't have + past participle; may slightly more likely and more formal than might; continuous forms must be raining, might have been playing).
- 19 Lesson 5: **The passive** (be + past participle in all tenses, modal passives, it is believed/said/thought/rumoured/considered/understood that..., questions and negatives, get passive less formal, no passive with intransitive verbs or state verbs like belong, suit, have).
- 20 Lesson 2: **Cleft sentences with what** (What makes me happy is..., What I like best is..., -ing after be when a verb is the focus: What makes me happy is spending time with my family; what-clause at the end: Meditating was what I enjoyed the most).
- 20 Lesson 5: **I wish and If only** (+ past simple for present wishes, + past perfect for past regrets; if only is stronger; for other people too: My brother wishes he was better at maths).
- 21 Lesson 2: **Separable, inseparable and three-part phrasal verbs** (object before or after particle; pronoun MUST go between: pick her up, not pick up her; inseparable: go over, come across; intransitive: go out, go up; three-part always inseparable: look forward to, listen out for, get along with; phrasal verbs as nouns and adjectives: go-ahead, broken-down; phrasal verbs are more informal than single-word equivalents: protect/look after, decline/turn down).
- 21 Lesson 5: **Order of adjectives** (opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose/type + noun; opinion before fact; "and" between two colour adjectives: black and white; commas between adjectives of the same type, no commas between different types: a large old leather suitcase; compound nouns like carving knife, sports jacket, rowing boat).
- 22 Lesson 2: **Mixed conditionals** (If + past perfect, would + base form: past condition, present result; If + past simple/continuous, would have + past participle: present condition, past result; might/could instead of would; teacher's rule: each clause carries a time marker, e.g. If I had studied engineering ten years ago, I would be an engineer now).
- 22 Lesson 5: **Present and past participle adjectives** (-ing = what causes the feeling; -ed = how a person feels; things do not have feelings: The book is interesting, not interested; confusing/confused, depressing/depressed, disappointing/disappointed, exciting/excited, frightening/frightened, interesting/interested, surprising/surprised, tempting/tempted, tiring/tired, worrying/worried, energized, inspiring, gripping, comforting, nurturing, satisfying, devastated, overwhelmed, intimidated, fascinating).
- 23 Lesson 2: **Giving advice: should, ought to, had better** (should most common; ought to more formal, rare in negatives and questions; had better = strong advice for a specific, immediate situation, danger if not followed, 'd better + base form, had better not; questions: Shouldn't you...?, Do you think we ought to...?, Hadn't we better...?; conditionals for advice; cleft: What you ought to do is...).
- 23 Lesson 5: **Future in the past** (would + base form; might/could; past continuous for arrangements; was/were going to for intentions; would be + -ing; would have + past participle (perfect); was/were supposed to; was/were to; was about to; was due to).
- 24 Lesson 1: **Verb patterns with gerunds and infinitives** (gerund only: anticipate, deny, despise, discuss, enjoy, admit, mind, avoid, consider, suggest; infinitive only: agree, ask, decide, expect, need, plan, want, afford, refuse, manage; both with little change: begin, continue, hate, like, love, start, prefer; change in meaning: stop, remember, forget, regret, try).
- 24 Lesson 5: **Conjunctions for comparing and contrasting** (whereas, while, whilst (old-fashioned); while on the contrary (total opposites), while in contrast, on the other hand (start of sentence or between commas), when in fact (corrects a false belief), but, although, though, but then, then again).
